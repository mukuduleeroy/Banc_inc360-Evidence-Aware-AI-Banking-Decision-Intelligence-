# Banc-Inc360 — complete 15-minute pitch

Use after the manual release/data/rehearsal gates in 10. This script does not assert that they have been cleared. Spoken paragraphs are blockquotes; stage directions are not spoken. Operator follows 04 using preloaded tabs and disposable rehearsal state. Aim for a measured 115–130 words/minute; live gestures occupy the remaining time. Rehearse to the absolute checkpoints, not by adding features or accelerating through caveats. A fully timed performance has **not** been rehearsed in Phase 11.

Opening visual: project title and the sentence “Customer evidence → explainable permissions → human review.” Do not use an official-bank affiliation claim or unverified university/organizer logo.

## 0:00–2:00 — problem and vision

**0:00–0:45 — title; no navigation.**

> Banc-Inc360 addresses a gap between transaction dashboards and decisions a relationship manager can defend. A bank can see activity falling, a relationship becoming more active, or a product apparently missing. But those observations are not yet permission to contact a customer or recommend a product. Is the history sufficient? Is the account eligible? Does the customer already hold the product? Can someone explain and review the proposed action?

**0:45–1:25 — introduce evidence/governance distinction.**

> Our prototype brings those questions into one inspectable workflow. It analyses historical transactions, presents customer intelligence, checks a proposed Savings discussion through six decision-integrity gates, and records a local human review with its evidence. We also demonstrate a separate hypothetical product upgrade and an explicit relationship-activity value proxy. We keep real source evidence, historical experiments and synthetic demonstrations visibly separate.

**1:25–2:00 — open Executive Overview; pause for audience.**

> The accepted extract contains 50,000 customer decisions. Of these, 2,052 are supported for staff review, 20 require caution and 47,928 withhold the proposed sales action. Those are permission outcomes under prototype rules—not predicted sales, bank approvals or a churn rate. The demonstration will show both an opportunity and why the system can refuse one. Our objective is better-supported decisions, not simply more recommendations.

Operator: hold counts. Speak clearly through “staff review.” Reach Customer Intelligence by 2:00.

Confirmed churn or relationship termination would need independent evidence. In this screen, filters help a manager inspect recency and history coverage without turning an observation into an unsupported probability.

## 5:00–8:00 — product opportunities and decision governance

**5:00–5:50 — Product Opportunity, Supported staff review, selected Savings explanation.**

> This is our implemented cross-sell: the customer has known other products, does not hold Savings, and has recorded qualifying deposit evidence. The proposed additional product is therefore distinct from something already held. All 2,052 supported cases have known existing products and no Savings. But a gap alone is not suitability, and these are prototype rules rather than an approved bank product policy. The application supports a discussion for review; it does not offer terms or initiate contact.

**5:50–7:15 — Decision Passport; point to six gates.**

> The Passport explains each control. G1 checks integrity and provenance. G2 checks whether history is sufficient: zero or one event withholds action; two or three require caution; four or more can pass. G3 prevents an unapproved fitted model from driving action. G4 checks current customer, account and KYC restrictions. G5 checks the supported product, ownership gap and behavioral rationale. G6 requires human review and prohibits automatic outreach. A blocking gate takes precedence over caution; a high activity score cannot rescue an ineligible case.

Operator: point to actual status/reason cards. Do not imply that the historical ML or hypothetical Premium package passed this Savings-specific G5.

**7:15–8:00 — explain abstention and business scope.**

> The 95.856 percent abstention share is sales-permission withholding, not AI uncertainty. Among those cases, 39,928 carry a current-status restriction code; other reasons include insufficient history, missing rationale and already-held Savings. These reasons overlap. We prefer an explicit reason for withholding to a confident-looking unsuitable offer. Supported means eligible for staff review—not commercially valuable, converted or bank-approved.

## 12:00–14:00 — AI, architecture and validation

**12:00–13:15 — historical comparison panel; show all three rows and cohorts.**

> Our historical experiment compares logistic regression and histogram boosting against recency, using five transaction-derived predictors. Logistic ROC-AUC is 0.965207; average precision is 0.982397. AUC is ranking discrimination—not 96.5 percent accuracy. All three methods reach Precision at 500 of one on this extract; that concerns inactivity labels, not retention or sales.

> January and July cohorts repeat customers; positive prevalence moves from about 19.44 to 59.33 percent. Cohort and outcome differences are verified, but economic causes, calibration and broader generalization are not. No fitted model or scaler is saved for live inference, so we do not show customer probabilities.
